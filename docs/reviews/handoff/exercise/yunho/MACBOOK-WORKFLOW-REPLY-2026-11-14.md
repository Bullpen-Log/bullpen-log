## Practical review of EVERY field — what an assessment must answer

1. **Video, name, source:** What exact movement is visible throughout? Is the Korean title and, for third-party video, the source title consistent with that variation and the registered video? Is it owned/reference-only/missing? Review framing, cuts and privacy, recording timestamps only when seen. Categorize as confirmed / needs correction / unverified.
2. **Equipment & installation:** Which physical objects are actually required/visible, and what is their name? What is the mounting point, body contact, tension/resistance, floor and clearance? Are they stable and is a listed item actually required or an alternative? The app’s multiple-equipment semantics may mean “all required”: verify before adding an alternative as a separate required item. An off-camera anchor, band support position or unseen alternative is not confirmed.
3. **Target body parts:** Compare the primary movement and active/stabilizing roles against displayed app categories. State which selected targets are appropriate, missing or unnecessary, and why; don't add every stabilizer as a primary target by default.
4. **Intensity vs difficulty:** Intensity concerns the intended demand / workload for scheduling; difficulty concerns coordination, setup and skill. Check assistance/resistance, range, speed, athlete ability and app definitions separately. Note when an app label is relative, a conservative programming judgment, or actually inconsistent. Do not infer less landing impact just because there is band assistance.
5. **How to perform / safety:** In simple, consistent Korean, explain equipment preparation → starting position → exact limb/body/direction placement → movement sequence and tempo → return/landing and repeat → realistic form and pain stop. Compare each action with the video, distinguish coaching guidance from a detail established visually, and make form stop conditional on it happening. For fast contact emphasize controlling landing rather than blindly maximizing height/speed. Avoid user-confusing unnecessary negatives and jargon; use paragraphs/line breaks.
6. **Purpose and pitching:** Explain why the underlying physical quality matters in pitching and how this particular movement trains it. Avoid “it can be used for training” as the whole explanation, and avoid claiming this exact exercise reproduces pitching or necessarily improves ball speed. Evidence strength determines wording.
7. **Sets, reps or hold, rest, per-side:** Check that counts match the nature of the drill and the app's meaning of one set. Decide whether time is blank for repetitions or vice versa; examine unilateral/pitching direction. Review fatigue and appropriate recovery for quality and the user's purpose; state starting values and plausible modifications, not an invented optimum. Check whether the equipment, method and prescription actually work together.
8. **User-facing report:** For every field give concise decision + reason, including unchanged fields. For every saved edit give exact before → after and why; where only a prior summary exists label it as such rather than reconstructing exact prose.
